the question

what does it mean to document something that's still alive?

the conventional approach to heritage documentation treats craft as artefact — something to be archived, preserved, and protected behind glass. this approach, however well-intentioned, has a fundamental flaw: it removes the work from the conditions that made it meaningful.

lippan art is not a relic. it continues to be made by women in villages across kutch. the question was never how to preserve it. the question was how to communicate its living intelligence to people who have never encountered it — and why they should care.

the research

six months of fieldwork. a hundred conversations. forty villages.

the research phase was immersive. i travelled to kutch, visited lippan artists in their homes, documented their techniques, and recorded oral histories that had never before been written down.

the motifs of lippan art are not decorative accidents. each one has a name, a meaning, a place in a larger symbolic system. the mirror fragments embedded in the clay catch light in specific ways at specific times of year — a kind of material calendar.

this understanding changed the design approach completely. the book could not simply show the work. it had to teach the reader how to see it.

the design

the book as translation — not archive.

every design decision in the book was made in service of one question: how do we help the reader understand this art the way its makers understand it?

the colour palette was drawn directly from kutchi mud, clay, and mirror. the typography used large white text on dark grounds — referencing the way lippan art creates light in dark spaces. the grid was modular, echoing the geometric systems of the motifs themselves.

chapter dividers were designed as full-bleed pattern studies — not decoration, but explanation. each showed a single motif broken into its components, helping readers understand the grammar before they encountered the full compositions.

the outcome

a publication that argues for the future of craft.

the final publication is 200 pages. it contains original fieldwork documentation, pattern analysis, oral history transcriptions, and contextual essays — all designed as a single, coherent reading experience.

most importantly: people who read it understand lippan art differently afterwards. they see its intelligence. that was the only goal.
